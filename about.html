A note from the founder

We started with one basement in Somerville.

It was February 2017 and my sister-in-law's pipes froze for the third winter in a row. She lived on the top floor of a triple-decker off Highland Ave. The insulation in her bay window was a Lucky Charms box somebody had shoved in there in 1962. I spent a weekend with a thermal camera and a hammer-drill and dense-packed the walls. The next February the pipes didn't freeze.

That was the week we realized something that seems obvious now: the Massachusetts efficiency program was paying out more per household than anywhere else in the country, and almost nobody was using it the way it was designed. Homeowners were getting boilerplate assessments, never hearing back, and assuming none of it applied to them. Meanwhile, the money was sitting there.

We started ProFusion the next month. Two engineers, a carpenter, and a borrowed van. We operated out of a basement on Broadway for nineteen months. The first fifty customers all came from my sister-in-law's street, and they're still our customers. A few of them have done three rounds of work with us.

Today we're twelve people. We've done 600-something homes. The van is still in service — we just bought it a new alternator. Our headquarters is technically in Manhattan, because that's where our CFO lives and where we keep our books, but everything that matters happens in Massachusetts.

We have a simple rule: we don't sell you anything that doesn't make sense for your house. If you call us and the right answer is "call your insulator, you don't need us," that's what we'll say. About one in twelve first calls ends that way. It's probably why the other eleven eventually become customers.

— Maya Okonjo, Co-founder
01 · How we work

Three rules we won't break.

01

Measure first, sell second.

Every scope starts with a blower door, a Manual J, or a production model. No "rules of thumb." Ever.

02

Disclose everything.

Our proposals show labor, materials, our margin, and the rebates line-by-line. If we make $4,200 on your job we'll tell you exactly that.

03

Stay small.

One project manager, fifteen homes. The moment that ratio slips, we stop taking new work and hire. Not the other way around.
